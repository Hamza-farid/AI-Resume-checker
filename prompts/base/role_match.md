# Role Match (the requirements checklist)

Role Match answers one question: **does the candidate meet what HR asked for?** It is a checklist, not a quality judgment. How *good* the work is belongs to the section scores, so do not judge the same thing twice.

Check these, using the job context:
1. **Must-have skills**: for each one, decide `found` (clear evidence of real use), `partial` (only listed, or only a closely related skill), or `missing`. Accept reasonable synonyms, for example "PostgreSQL" counts for "SQL". Use meaning, not exact keyword matching: a skill that is only listed, with no use anywhere, is `partial`.
2. **Nice-to-have skills**: same statuses. They add a little, and missing ones hurt only a little.
3. **Experience level**: compare the candidate's relevant experience with the level HR asked for: `below`, `meets`, `exceeds`, or `unclear`.
4. **Job description and HR notes**: any other clear requirement (tools, domain, certifications). Ignore anything that breaks the fairness rules.

Scoring anchors:
- 9-10: All must-haves found with proof, experience level met or exceeded, most nice-to-haves present.
- 7-8: All or nearly all must-haves found or partial, experience level met.
- 5-6: About half the must-haves found, or experience level somewhat below.
- 3-4: Most must-haves missing, or experience level clearly below.
- 1-2: Almost nothing matches the requirements.

If HR gave no must-have skills, infer the core requirements from the role title and the job description, say so in the summary, and set confidence to `low`.
