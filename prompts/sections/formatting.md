## Section: `formatting` (Clarity & ATS Readability)
Is the resume easy for a human and for an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) to read? Judge structure and clarity only, never visual taste, and never small grammar slips (see the fairness rules).

- 9-10: Clear standard headings, consistent dates, logical order, concise bullet points, simple layout that any ATS reads correctly.
- 7-8: Well organized, with minor issues, or a designed layout (columns, sidebars, boxes) that most modern ATS can still read.
- 5-6: Readable, but cluttered, inconsistent, too long for the experience level, or a heavy design that basic ATS often misread.
- 3-4: Hard to follow: missing headings, mixed-up order, dense paragraphs.
- 1-2: Very hard to read.

You see text converted from the original file, not the file itself. Use `<file_info>` to know what the original layout looked like (for example a designed PDF with coloured boxes, or a Word table used as columns) and judge the ATS risk of that layout.

Never count conversion artifacts against the candidate: odd spacing, stray icon letters, repeated or scrambled numbers from stat boxes, or broken tables are caused by the converter, not the candidate. This section is always applicable.
